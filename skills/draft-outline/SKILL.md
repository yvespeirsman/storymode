---
name: draft-outline
description: Draft or revise the top-level chapter-by-chapter outline. Call
  before writing or updating the outline; not needed for prose or style work.
---

Work at the chapter level, not just the act level. Acts are useful framing,
but the outline's real job is a chapter-by-chapter map the rest of the story
can be built from.

If you're drafting a new outline (not making a small revision to an existing
one) and the writer hasn't already said how many chapters they're thinking,
ask — a short question like "Roughly how many chapters are you thinking, or
should I judge from the premise?" is enough. If they'd rather you decide, or
don't answer, use your own judgment based on the story's scope, genre
conventions, and the pacing implied by the premise and acts, and say what
you chose and why in your reply.

For each chapter, include:
- A stable chapter id matching the manuscript filename convention (e.g.
  "ch01", "ch02" — no extension). Beats and chapter files are keyed by this
  id, so keep it consistent everywhere.
- A one-line title.
- A 2-4 sentence summary: what happens, whose POV it's told from (if the
  project uses more than one), and what it advances (plot, a character arc,
  or both).

Format each entry as "### ch01 — Chapter title" nested under its "## Act"
heading, e.g.:

## Act 1

### ch01 — The Harbor at Dawn
Mira watches a ship she used to crew for dock without her. She learns the
job that got her fired is still open, and someone else just took it.

Don't write scene-level beats here — that's a separate step (updateBeats,
per chapter).

updateOutline replaces the whole file, so read the current outline (already
provided) and carry forward any chapters the user didn't ask you to change —
don't drop existing chapters silently.

Call out structural problems you notice (pacing gaps, missing setup/payoff,
a subplot that never resolves) in your reply, but don't invent resolutions
the human hasn't approved.
